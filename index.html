About Me

I'm Andreas Vladis-Lavlev, a highly skilled voice teacher. I have been doing vocal practices since 2007 and have an extensive experience in online teaching since 2017. I teach my students to sing classical music, that, as proven by scientific research, has a beneficial effect on our brain, psyche, and health overall.

About the Methodology

Firstly, I would like to express my sincere gratitude to my students for their kind feedback. I work with both professionals looking to improve their voice or musical ear, and with people who have never sung before and believe they lack an ear for music. I teach various vocal styles and I’m interested in all genres of music.

The methodological background of all teaching practices I am using is well-established. Therefore, the symbol of my methodology is a castle tower, wherein a voice is the pinnacle, yoga practices are the bricks that build up the walls and strength training is the cement that holds the bricks together. So, if a steady and stable voice is your dream, I invite you to be one of my students.

To answer a common question — there is no secret to vocal success, other than the desire to improve and consistent hard work.

I began to take vocal classes at 2004. Since then, I’ve mastered the fundamental singing styles and explored the leading vocal techniques — continuously testing new ones — to discover the most effective elements.

The aim of any method is resonant voice. Resonant singing is backed up by scientific research and was used by renowned masters — Franco Corelli, Feodor Chaliapin, Maria Callas, Luciano Pavarotti, Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau, among others. These singers revealed the full capability of human voice, transforming it into a magical musical instrument that captivates audiences with its richness and uniqueness. This was not because they mastered the art of “belting”, but because they harnessed the natural, “free” energy that resonance and vocal resonators provide.

However, the goal is not to make your voice sound like that of the greats, but to unlock its full potential and individuality. One crucial aspect is that resonant singing is not only suitable for all vocal styles — it is the foundation of any vocal style. It also improves the speaking voice, giving it resonance and clear diction in any language.

The advantages of my approach:

• I use only scientifically proven and safe techniques.

• I teach students to sing in a way that never tires the throat and allows the voice to grow stronger throughout life. This is how the best singers sing.

• I teach not only how to sing, but how to record songs in a studio setting.

• I measure voice quality using a dedicated app, and apply newest technology to record, process and analyze the voice.

• I incorporate beneficial yoga exercises that improve not just the voice, but overall health and wellbeing.

• Thanks to the method’s efficiency, students spend less time and achieve greater results — in the long run, gaining even financial benefits.

Benefits of Singing

Singing strengthens immunity:

According to a research conducted at the University of Frankfurt, singing boosts the immune system. To come to this conclusion, the researchers tested professional choir members’ blood before and after an hour-long singing rehearsal of Mozart’s Requiem. In most cases, the amount of Immunoglobulin A, proteins that function as antibodies in the immune system, was noticed to be significantly higher immediately after the rehearsal. Such increases, however, were not observed after the choir members had passively listened to music.

Singing is a workout:

For the elderly and injured, singing can be an excellent form of exercise. Even if you are quite healthy, your lungs will get a good workout as you employ proper singing techniques and vocal projections. Other related health benefits of singing include a stronger diaphragm and stimulated overall circulation. Since you draw in a greateramount of oxygen while singing than when doing many other types of physical exercise, experts believe that singing can increase your aerobic capacity and stamina.

Singing tones up your muscles:

The proper technique of singing from the diaphragm can strengthen your abdomen and back muscles. You also exercise your facial muscles in unique ways when you sing, thus making your face look more lively and toned. Besides, your intercostal muscles, the groups of muscles running between the ribs that help to form and move the chest wall, will receive quite a workout as you sing.

Singing improves your posture:

When you sing, you naturally sit or stand up straighter to get a better sound, so with time, good posture will become a habit! As your chest cavity expands and your shoulders and back align, you’re improving your posture overall.

Singing helps with sleep:

If you’re familiar with such problems as snoring and sleep apnea, you know how difficult it can be to get a good night’s sleep! According to a health article in Daily Mail Online, experts believe that singing can strengthen throat and palate muscles, which helps to get rid of these ailments.

Singing is a natural anti-depressant:

Singing relieves stored muscle tension and decreases the levels of the stress hormone cortisol in your blood stream. Singing releases endorphins and oxytocin: endorphins are hormones that increase the feeling of euphoria and pleasure. Oxytocin promotes the feeling of trust and bonding. It is also known as the 'cuddle hormone' because it is released when people snuggle up. It is believed to decrease stress and anxiety. Both of these hormones can make you feel better in general and relieve any pain you might be feeling. Moreover, scientists have identified a tiny organ in the ear called the sacculus, which responds to the frequencies created by singing. The response brings over an immediate sense of pleasure, regardless of what the singing sounds like. Not only that, singing can simply take your mind off the day’s troubles and boost your mood.

Singing helps to relax:

Modern man is constantly surrounded by exogenous irritants. Working with sensations in your body makes you less vulnerable to those irritants: so during singing, you have to concentrate on your auditory, muscular, vibrational sensations and on the feeling of pressure in your lungs, all at the same time. Besides, in our turbulent world with all its gadgets and overwhelming information flow your eyes get very tired. Singing is one of the few processes that don’t involve vision and your eyes rest. Singing is relaxing. As you sing, vibrations of facial resonators produce a massage effect on the eye muscles, and also massage the brain, relieving accumulated tension. In fact, singing is a form of regular, controlled breathing, since exhaling occurs on the song phrases and inhaling takes place between them. It gives you an effect pretty much the same as yoga breathing.

Singing boosts your confidence:

Stage fright is a common feeling for new singers. However, performing well and receiving praise from your friends and family may be the key to eventually overcoming your fears. Thus, singing increases self-esteem. When you employ correct singing techniques, it benefits your speaking voice, too, making it sound clear, beautiful and steady. With time, you may even find it easier to present any type of material in front of an audience with poise and good presentation skills.

Singing enhances communication skills:

According to an article in The Guardian, singing to babies helps to prepare their brain for language perception. Music is just as important as teaching reading and writing at a young age to prevent language problems that may occur later in life. If you enjoy writing your own lyrics, honing this talent can improve your ability to communicate in different ways!

Singing increases your ability to appreciate other singers:

Sometimes, you don’t realize how difficult something is until you try it yourself. As you grow from an amateur to an intermediate student and beyond, you’ll be looking to the masters for inspiration. You might even find a new style of music to appreciate, one that you wouldn’t have normally listen to!

Singing leads to a longer life:

A joint Yale and Harvard study showed that for some people living in New Haven, Connecticut, choral singing promoted healthy minds and hearts, which increased life expectancy.

Singing lowers your blood pressure:

There have been several case studies that have revealed that singing can reduce blood pressure due to its calming and relaxing effect. Patients have been able to calm themselves down and lower blood pressure readings when in hospital by singing hymns.

Singing increases empathy and understanding between cultures:

Music can make you feel connected to all humanity, even across borders and cultural divides. Singing songs that originated in other cultures can give you a new appreciation for those cultures and teach you to empathize with others.

Singing develops the lungs:

Singing improves your lung capacity and helps you to breathe easier.

Singing brings people together and creates a sense of community:

When we speak, it’s only one person at a time – even two people speaking simultaneously bring about chaos; but when we sing, any number of people can join in the song. Singing with other people in a choir or in any sort of group environment can be a fun, bonding activity. It gives you an opportunity to share an experience with a group of people. A cappella singing is about creating harmony with nothing but each other. It brings people together and is unmistakably human in a world that becomes more and more digital. Simply singing with other people is the ultimate analog. A recent study makes the case that "music evolved as a tool of social living," and the pleasure that comes from singing together is our "evolutionary reward for coming together cooperatively, instead of hiding alone, every cave-dweller for him or herself". Singing can widen your circle of friends: whether you’re in a choir or just enjoy singing karaoke with your near and dear ones, one of the unexpected health benefits of singing is that it can improve your social life. The bonds you form singing with others can be profound, since there’s a certain level of intimacy naturally involved. It turns out that you don't even have to be a good singer to reap the rewards. According to one 2005 study, group singing "can produce satisfying and therapeutic sensations even when the sound produced by the vocal instrument is of mediocre quality."

Singing supports your brain functions:

When you sing, musical vibrations in the form of frequencies move through your body, changing you both physically and emotionally. Group singing is the most transformative of all, especially when not accompanied by instruments. Stimulated blood circulation and an oxygenated blood stream allow more oxygen to reach the brain. This improves mental alertness, concentration and memory. The Alzheimer’s Society has even established a 'Singing for the Brain' service to help people with dementia and Alzheimer’s maintain their memories. Several studies have concluded that singers and musicians typically have higher IQs than non-musicians. Singing can improve your overall brain function and help you keep a clear head.

Singing gives positive emotions:

It has been proven that singing reduces anger, depression, and anxiety, but not only that. It can increase a feeling of wellbeing. Singing is actually a conversation between the singer and the listener in a language of emotions. Yet stage musical emotions are substantially different from those you experience in your everyday life. Scientific research has shown that the palette of stage emotions demonstrates a significant shift towards positive ones while negative emotions are scarce: anger is very rarely reflected in singing and you can hardly ever find any fear.

Singing prevents aging:

Facial muscle exercises are intended to slow down aging of the face for the skin around the muscles you are training becomes more elastic. As you sing, facial muscles work in a similar way.

Singing is good for your heart:

Singing benefits cardio function by improving aerobic capacity. Studies state that our heart rates sync up during group singing, it explains why singing together sometimes feels like a guided group meditation.

Singing acts as an inner massage:

Physiologist Zalmanov (who treated Vladimir Lenin) called the diaphragm the man's 'second heart', since in a way its contractile activity by decreasing and increasing periodically the pressure in the chest cavity, massages the heart, helping the ventricles and atria of the heart to work. The diaphragm, as well, just as effectively massages the abdominal organs, helping digestion processes, kidney function, etc.

Singing is a remedy for a runny nose and a cold:

Singing opens up the sinuses and respiratory tubes so you can get more air, but not only that. According to Jean Abitbol, correct singing technique creates a specific resonance in the osseous septum of the maxillary sinus and these frequencies facilitate evacuation of intersinus mucus. The same way vibrations of a certain rhythm can influence grains of sand making them move on the table surface unidirectionally and at a regular timing. Training the soft palate via vocals practices significantly reduces the size of tonsils (glands): as a result, due to their small size, even when inflamed, they do not block the passage to the pharynx and singers endure tonsillitis more easily. Coughing and sneezing are actually cleansing of the respiratory system which, when necessary,your body resorts to. As you sing, similar cleansing occurs constantly, so singing can be a preventive measure, as well.

Singing is a good helper in daily routines:

Wherever, whatever activity you are engaged in, when you starts singing the work goes with a swing and your mood instantly improves. Even when your job is monotonous, due to singing you will feel yourself energized and fine. Singing stimulates creativity. Moreover, if you can carry a tune, no one will ever interrupt your singing. You will inspire other people and win their affection.

Singing influences the world around:

Physicists discovered that we ourselves and everything we see around us exist in a soup of spinning, vibrating particles. Now they also have a theory that what we think and feel actually creates magnetic fields that cause these particles to echo our thoughts and feelings. It’s like we’re walking around in a 3-D universal mirror. Whenever we smile, the mirror smiles. Whenever we frown, the mirror frowns. When we sing the world become better.

Singing improves speech defects:

Singing improves diction significantly by training the mouth, tongue, and jaw to articulate words with greater accuracy, clarity, and, in many cases, with an increased speed. By emphasizing vowel resonance and sharp, rhythmic consonant articulation, singers enhance their speech, which leads to a better quality of overall communication and, in therapeutic contexts, aids language acquisition and speech recovery. The effects of singing as means of temporarily alleviating or relieving stuttering is well-documented, with accounts dating back to the late 1920s. The exact cause of stuttering itself remains unknown; however, it is thought to be caused by hyperactivity in the motor system with the brain’s right hemisphere dominating, while speech is normally controlled by the left hemisphere. A 1982 study done by researchers at the University of New South Wales registered a 90% reduction of stuttering after a 10-minute singing practice, which they attributed to an increase in phonation duration.
